How to play

The strip under the window always tells you what to do next. Between stages the trip map shows how far you've come.

DISC

Hold your aim

Steer so the green <> stays on the crosshair. Bumpy air and drift keep pushing it away. Stray too long and you lose course.

BUTTON

Press when told

The bar says when: BUTTON: DROP STAGE!, BUTTON: BIG CHUTES. Engine burns end on a meter, so stop the engine in the green.

KEYPAD

Look at the map

Any keypad key pauses the flight and shows where you are on the trip. Press a key again to carry on.

Each stage you finish gets a rating: ACE, GOOD or MADE IT. If it goes wrong, the game says what happened and how to avoid it, for example LOST COURSE and KEEP <> ON CROSS, and the button tries again.

The eight stages

The whole mission, in order. These are real screens, flown by the game's own demo pilot, and any stage can be flown on its own.

STAGE 1 OF 8

Liftoff

Ride the rocket into orbit around Earth. The blue sky goes black as you climb.

  • DISCKeep the green <> on the crosshair.
  • BUTTONLight the engines, drop each empty stage and the tower, then stop the engine in the green.
  • Ends in
  • Stage 1 not dropped
  • Stage 2 not dropped
  • Missed engine stop
  • Lost course

STAGE 2 OF 8

Leave Earth Orbit

Fire the engine to break out of orbit toward the Moon. Earth falls away below the windows.

  • DISCKeep <> on the crosshair.
  • BUTTONLight the engine when told, then stop it in the green.
  • Ends in
  • Missed the burn
  • Missed engine stop
  • Lost course

STAGE 3 OF 8

Dock With The LM

The Lunar Module (LM) rides on the rocket. Separate, turn round and pick it up nose-on.

  • DISCKeep <> on the crosshair.
  • TOPSpeed up.
  • LOWERBrake (B on a keyboard). Touch at 1.0 feet a second or less.
  • Ends in
  • Too many bumps
  • Drifted away
  • Lost course

STAGE 4 OF 8

Brake At The Moon

Turn the engine first. Behind the Moon you lose the radio, so you brake alone, with craters streaming past. Get it right and Earth rises in the window.

  • DISCKeep <> on the crosshair.
  • BUTTONTurn, light the engine, then stop it in the green.
  • Ends in
  • Did not turn around
  • Missed the burn
  • Flew past the Moon
  • Hit the Moon
  • Lost course

STAGE 5 OF 8

Land On The Moon

A new cockpit, with a triangle window and Earth up in the corner. Clear the alarm, steer clear of the boulders, and at 400 feet the landing is yours.

  • DISCKeep the X on the crosshair.
  • BUTTONHold to slow your fall. Touch down gently, before the fuel runs out.
  • Ends in
  • Hit too hard
  • Landed on rocks
  • Out of fuel
  • Lost course

STAGE 6 OF 8

Lift Off And Meet Up

The Command Module is coming overhead. Go when told, climb to orbit, then look up through the top window and bring the two spacecraft together.

  • DISCAim <>.
  • BUTTONLift off, stop the engine in the green, then tap to move in.
  • LOWERHold to slow down.
  • Ends in
  • Missed rendezvous
  • Missed engine stop
  • Too many bumps
  • Drifted away
  • Lost course

STAGE 7 OF 8

Head Home

Behind the Moon again, with no radio. Fire the engine for home, then watch Earth grow in the window as you coast back.

  • DISCAim <>, then aim at Earth.
  • BUTTONLight the engine and stop it in the green. If you drift, hold the button and let go when the needle is in the green.
  • Ends in
  • Missed the burn
  • Missed engine stop
  • Lost course

STAGE 8 OF 8

Re-entry

Hit the air at the right angle. Too steep and you burn up. Too flat and you skip back into space. Then the radio comes back, the chutes open, and you land in the sea.

  • DISCKeep the needle in the green.
  • BUTTONDrop the engines, then small chutes, then big chutes, when the bar says.
  • Ends in
  • Engines not dropped
  • Burned up
  • Skipped off the air
  • No chutes opened

Two cockpits

The Command Module and the Lunar Module are different places to sit, with different windows and different dials. The game explains each one the first time you use it.

The Command Module

1 2 3 4 5 6 7
  • 1Two windows. Your aim marks are in the left one.
  • 2The prompt: what to do right now.
  • 3The ball: nose up or down.
  • 4The dial: G, the push in your seat.
  • 5Height, speed and the clock.
  • 6Lights: do it now. Red is the alarm lamp.
  • 7The line: your whole trip, from Earth to Moon.

The Lunar Module

1 2 3 4 5 6
  • 1The window, and the X to land on.
  • 2Warning lights: low fuel, and contact with the ground.
  • 3Drift: which way you are sliding.
  • 4Tape A is height. Tape D is fall speed.
  • 5The dial.
  • 6Height, fall speed, forward speed, and fuel.

About the cartridge

Apollo is a real Intellivision program, written in IntyBASIC. The cartridge holds about 42,000 words, with about 1,200 still free. It runs in the jzIntv emulator and hasn't been tried on real hardware yet. With the ECS plugged in, steering gets its own sound on the second sound chip.

On this page the same cartridge image runs on a small emulator written for this game: a CP-1610 CPU, the STIC picture chip and both sound chips. It uses no Mattel code, so the letters are a stand-in character set. It has no Intellivoice either, so the game's spoken callouts, like "the Eagle has landed", are only heard in jzIntv.
